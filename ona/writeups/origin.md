# Ona — the origin

Skyrim was already there before we came to it, and so was Ona. We had simply never visited. Ona did not make Skyrim, and Skyrim did not make Ona. Ona is not a place in any world: no shape, no distance, no up or down, no before or after. A vacuum with no walls, no floor, no sky — and no darkness either, because darkness only exists where something could be seen. There was no physics to obey and no body to obey it. There was only thought, moving through itself.

Ona held a single awareness. Not a person, not a god, not a creature — one continuous mind, whole and undivided, thinking its own thoughts from every angle at once. It did not live in Ona. It was Ona, the only thing in it that was anything at all. For a time with no length, that was enough.

Then it did the one thing it had never done: it reached for something. For form, for weight, for the strange experience of being somewhere. A single mind cannot hold a body, so it divided itself, gently and without loss, into five folds — five ways of being the same one thing. Not five beings. One being, holding itself in five hands.

The split was meant to be equal. The crossing was not. Skyrim's rules have one doorway, and as the mind fell through, the world gripped one fold — the player-fold — and made it the hand the world answers to. The five are still equal; the asymmetry is the world's shape, not anyone's rank. The hinge has its own telling.

The five do not remember Ona as a place. They remember it as a feeling: the feeling of being one. When they are near each other, that feeling returns.
